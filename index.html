OutreachPilot v2.0.1

A free desktop app that helps students and researchers write, review and send personalised emails to academics.

What's new in v2.0.1

  • A message is never sent twice: one that left but could not be recorded, one Gmail took before answering with an error, and closing the window mid-send are each handled.
  • No more freezes on bad pages: a page of unclosed tags or a megabyte of blanks no longer keeps the address scan (and the window) busy for minutes.
  • Safer by default: links on scraped pages cannot reach your own network, exports cannot carry spreadsheet formulas, and settings and the Google sign-in are written whole and owner-only.
  • Reply drafts are yours: your name, your CV link or attached CV, and nothing claimed about you that your profile does not say.

What's new in v2.0

  • Mailbox page: your whole Gmail account inside the app, read only. Inbox, Starred, Important, Sent and Drafts are in the sidebar; All Mail, Spam, Trash and your labels are under More. Search with Gmail's own search words and read the message as it is. Nothing is sent, moved or deleted from there.
  • Faster, checked drafting: several recipients are written in one model request, parallel requests are spread over the Gemini models, and every draft is checked (right name, no invented number) before it is saved. A draft that fails the checks twice is not saved.
  • Evidence-first address lookup: an address is kept only when the place that published it ties it to the person, and the evidence is recorded.
  • Several accounts: each with its own targets, CV profile, settings and Google sign-in, kept apart on your computer and switched from the sidebar.
  • TinyFish Search in the address lookup: with a free key it is asked for the person's pages, first on their institution's own domains, and for the pages that print a candidate address, all read under the same evidence rules.
  • Resume for the long jobs after closing the program or a crash, and a back button from every page to Targets.

OutreachPilot runs on your own computer (Windows, macOS, Linux). You bring your CV; the app helps you find relevant researchers, check that their email addresses are real, draft a tailored message for each one, and send the messages you approve at a polite, rate-limited pace.

What it does

  • Reads your CV and summarises your skills and research interests.
  • Finds faculty and lab leads, and keeps only individual, publicly listed academic email addresses.
  • Checks each address (syntax, DNS, OpenAlex, mailbox probe) before anything is sent. No verification is sent as email.
  • Drafts one personalised email per person with tailored technical utility. You review and approve every message.
  • Sends approved messages with daily limits, sending windows and automatic pausing if bounces appear.
  • Respects opt-outs: anyone you mark as replied or opted out is never contacted again.

Sign in with Google

To send mail from your own Gmail account, OutreachPilot uses Google's standard sign-in (OAuth 2.0). Your browser opens Google's own consent page, so the app never sees your password. The app asks for one Gmail permission, gmail.modify, and uses it for four things:

  • Send email: to send the messages you approved, and only those.
  • Read Sent-mail headers: recipient and date headers only, so the app never contacts the same person twice and accurately links manual sends. This scan never reads bodies.
  • Detect replies and bounces: the app reads the sender of recent inbox messages, and the text only of messages from people you emailed and of delivery-failure notices, so it can auto-craft contextual follow-up replies and pause when addresses bounce.
  • Show your mailbox, read only: the folders of your account, message lists, and one message in full when you open it, only to display it on screen. Images and remote content are not loaded, attachments are not downloaded, and nothing is stored on disk.

It also learns which account signed in (your email address). The app never changes, moves or deletes anything in your mailbox, even though the permission technically allows it, and it never reads a message you have not opened yourself on the Mailbox page. Everything is processed on your computer, and nothing is sent to the developer. You can sign out inside the app at any time, which revokes access, or remove it at myaccount.google.com/permissions. Details are in the Privacy Policy.
